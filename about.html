Background

I'm a Computer Engineering undergraduate at Purdue University, concentrating in computer systems. My interests sit at the boundaries most curricula step over: ASIC design and computer architecture, compilers, operating systems and networks, numerical analysis, and the quantitative machinery underneath markets.

The through-line is a refusal to use abstractions I haven't opened. I've written a Unix shell against raw POSIX calls rather than a library, a TCP server against raw sockets rather than a framework, and a simulation engine whose linear algebra I derived by hand before typing a line of it. It is a slow way to learn and the only one that has ever worked for me.

Most of what I build starts as a question I couldn't answer from a diagram. What does the kernel actually do with a signal. Why does a finite grid reproduce d'Alembert's solution when it has never heard of d'Alembert. Where exactly does a GPU stop being fast. The projects are the residue of chasing those down.
